How the calculation works

Capital gains — and why losses don't help you

XTB Romania is a local intermediary, so it computes and withholds the tax on every sale at source. The rate depends only on how long you held that particular lot: lower if you held it 365 days or more, higher if less. The tax is final — you don't re-declare the gain for income tax.

The catch, and it is a big one: the tax is applied per winning transaction. A loss on another trade does not reduce it. Sell one position for +10.000 lei and another for −10.000 lei in the same year and you are flat in your account but you still pay tax on the 10.000 lei gain — and the loss cannot be carried forward either.

The same asymmetry applies to CASS: with a Romanian intermediary, the amount that counts toward the CASS thresholds is the gross gain, not your net result.

This is exactly backwards from the usual "harvest your losses in December" advice, which works at a foreign broker (where you self-declare on the net gain) but does nothing for you here. At XTB Romania the only real lever is which sales you make, and when.

Converting to RON

ANAF works in lei, and so does your broker when it withholds. Each leg of a trade is converted at the rate of its own date: the purchase at the rate on the day you bought, the sale at the rate on the day you sold. That means a gain in RON can be larger than your gain in EUR when the leu weakens between the two dates — and it can even be a RON gain on a EUR loss.

Three modes are available. BNR annual average uses one rate per calendar year (the standard reference for foreign income in the D212) and works offline. Daily rates fetches a daily series from the European Central Bank and matches each trade date — closest to what your broker actually did, but it needs an internet connection and ECB and BNR rates differ by a fraction. Fixed rate uses one number you type, which is the simplest and the least accurate.

Dividends and interest are converted at the annual average, which is the convention for foreign income in the declaration.

CASS — the cliff-edge contribution

CASS on passive income is not a percentage of what you earn. It is 10% of a fixed base, and which base applies depends on which threshold your total passive income crosses. The thresholds are 6, 12 and 24 gross minimum wages.

Because it works in steps, one leu can cost you thousands. Land one leu under a threshold and you owe nothing extra; land one leu over it and the whole step is due. This is the single most valuable thing to watch in December.

What goes into the pot: rent (after the standard deduction), dividends (net of the tax withheld), interest (net), crypto gains, gains at a foreign broker (net), gross gains at a Romanian broker, and other sources. What stays out: salary and pension — those are covered by their own CASS, and paying CASS twice on passive income is normal, not a bug.

Being employed does not exempt you. If your passive income crosses the threshold, you owe this on top of the CASS deducted from your payslip.

Dividends from abroad and the foreign tax credit

Dividends from foreign shares are not taxed at source by a Romanian payer, so you declare them yourself in the D212 and pay the Romanian rate on the gross amount. Tax already paid abroad becomes a credit against that — but only up to the rate agreed in the double-taxation treaty with that country. For the US that is 10%.

If your broker withholds the full US statutory 30% instead of the treaty 10%, the extra 20% is simply lost: Romania will not credit it and the US will not refund it automatically. The fix is a W-8BEN form with your broker, which takes minutes and applies to all future dividends. The Tax tab flags this automatically if it sees an effective rate above 15%.

Filing the D212 (Declarația Unică)

The declaration for a given year is filed by 25 May of the following year, online through the ANAF SPV. What you actually fill in:

  • Capital gains through XTB Romania: nothing for income tax — the tax was already withheld and is final.
  • Foreign dividends and interest: Chapter I, Section 2, Subsection 1 — one block per country, with the gross income, the tax paid abroad and the credit claimed.
  • CASS: Chapter I, Section 3, Subsection 2 — the base you landed on and the 10%.
  • Rent, crypto, other sources: their own sections in Chapter I.

Chapter II is a separate matter: it is where you opt in to pay CASS voluntarily for the current year when you are below the thresholds and not otherwise insured.

There has historically been a small bonus for paying early, and it applies only to the income tax, never to CASS. Check whether it is still in force for your year before counting on it.

Reading your XTB documents

Fișă de portofoliu — the annual statement of gains and losses per country, in RON, with the tax withheld. This is what ANAF sees. Type it into section 1 of the Tax tab and tick the override box.

Raport dividende și dobânzi — gross dividends, tax withheld at source, net, plus interest paid on your free cash. Cross-check it against what the import found.

Closed positions export — per-trade detail: open date, close date, purchase and sale value. This is what lets the tool split gains by holding period and estimate the RON conversion. Export the whole calendar year.

Cash operations — dividends, withholding tax, free-funds interest and the "RO tax" lines showing exactly what XTB withheld in lei. The tool reads the RON amount out of those lines and reconciles it against its own estimate.

Privacy, sharing and where your data lives

Everything runs in your browser. The file never uploads your positions anywhere; it stores them in this browser's local storage so they survive a refresh. The only outbound request is the optional exchange-rate lookup, which sends no personal data.

If you share this file with family or friends, share the file, not your browser — they will open it empty and import their own export. Nothing personal is baked into the HTML.

Where these rules come from

Law 227/2015 (the Fiscal Code) as amended — notably Law 141/2025, which raised the investment tax rates and the dividend rate from 1 January 2026 — plus the ANAF instructions for the D212 and the published minimum-wage decisions that set the CASS thresholds. Exchange rates: bnr.ro is authoritative; the live lookup here uses the European Central Bank daily series, which is close but not identical. Everything in this file was assembled in August 2026 — if you are reading it much later, check the rates table above against the current law before trusting the output.
